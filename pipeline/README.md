# GCAT demand pipeline

Bottom-up launch demand for the Isar Aerospace / Andøya case, built from Jonathan McDowell's General Catalog of Artificial Space Objects (GCAT). Generated by `pipeline/gcat_demand.py`; do not edit by hand, rerun the script.

```bash
python3 -I pipeline/gcat_demand.py --raw <raw-dir> --out dashboard/src/data/generated --download
```

Raw files stay outside the repo (`--raw`). `--download` fetches missing files, `--refresh` all of them; `--audit-csv` writes the per-payload table used for the checks below.

## Method and rules

**Population.** Payloads (SatType byte 1 `P`) with launch date (GCAT `LDate`, UTC) in the year from the four orbital object catalogs: `satcat` (SATCAT 1–69,999), `satcat100k` (100,000+), `auxcat` (orbital objects without a SATCAT entry) and `tmpcat` (in orbit, number pending). Objects that failed to reach orbit sit in `ftocat` and are only counted for reporting. Each JCAT occurs once in these catalogs (later phases are in `ecat`), so each payload is counted once.

Excluded (count per year below):
- `alias-phase`: SatType 'PA' alias/lease phase record
- `non-standard-PX`: SatType 'PX' non-standard payload (carrier stacks, attached capsules, simulators)
- `launch-failure`: launch coded as orbital failure in launch.tsv (LaunchCode OF...)
- `marginal-launch`: marginal/suborbital launch tag (not yyyy-nnn, e.g. Starship 'yyyy-Unn')
- `suborbital-orbit`: canonical orbit missing, suborbital or trans-atmospheric (OpOrbit ATM/SO/TA)
- `duplicate`: duplicate Piece+Name across catalogs

**ISS and carrier deployments.** GCAT dates them by the launch that carried them (cargo flight's `LDate` and `Launch_Tag`, although the piece name is 1998-067xx). They count in that launch year with their own canonical orbit; payloads without a usable orbit (still inside or attached) take the orbit of the parent object (ISS, deployer, carrier), walking up the `Parent` chain.

**Orbit class** (canonical orbit `Perigee`/`Apogee`/`Inc`/`OpOrbit`):

| Class | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Beyond | Status `DSO`/`DSA`, OpOrbit CLO/EEO/HCO/PCO/SSE/DSO, or deep-space flag (SatType byte 5 or 12 = `D`) with apogee ≥ 2,000 km |
| SSO (Polar/SSO) | apogee < 2,000 km and inclination ≥ 85° (incl. the few retrograde LEO orbits above 110.6°) |
| LEO | apogee < 2,000 km and inclination < 85° |
| GTO/GEO | apogee ≥ 2,000 km and OpOrbit GEO\*/GTO/HEO\*/VHEO, or apogee ≥ 30,000 km (GEO-bound transfer/drift), or eccentricity ≥ 0.5, or period ≥ 23 h |
| MEO | every other Earth orbit with apogee ≥ 2,000 km |

Andøya band: inclination within [90°, 110.6°] (licence corridor 280–360°).

**Mass.** GCAT `Mass` = wet mass of the object at orbit insertion without attached objects. `TotMass` is not used: it bundles attached objects (cargo, trunks, mass simulators, sometimes the upper stage), so summing it would double-count. Masses flagged `?` are McDowell's estimates (about ±20 %) and are kept. Missing masses would be imputed by the median of the same psatcat `Program`, then `Bus`, then lead owner, then orbit class × customer group, then all payloads. Mass classes have inclusive upper bounds (≤50, 50–200, 200–700, 700–1,000, 1,000–2,000, >2,000 kg), so 700 kg fits Spectrum's SSO and 1,000 kg its LEO capacity.

Imputation back-test (no masses were missing, so the fallback is validated instead): masking 100 random non-captive payloads (seed 7) and re-imputing them reproduces their total mass to 99.0% and the mass class for 95 of 100 (levels used: imputed:bus 17, imputed:orbit+group 2, imputed:program 81).

**Customer type** (first matching rule wins). `Owner` is a `/`-separated list of org codes, the first is the lead operator; org class (A academic, B business, C civil government, D defence) and `UCode` come from `orgs.tsv`; owner state is the object's `State`; payload class is psatcat `Class` (McDowell's per-satellite judgement).

| Rule | Assignment |
| --- | --- |
| `starlink-owner` | captive/Starlink: lead owner UCode SPXS (SpaceX Starlink) |
| `starshield-bus` | captive/Starlink: Bus 'Starshield' (NRO proliferated, SpaceX-built and -launched) |
| `kuiper-oneweb` | captive/Kuiper / OneWeb: owner UCode KUIP or ONEWEB, or Program Kuiper/OneWeb |
| `china-state` | captive/China: State CN/HK/MO |
| `russia-state` | captive/Russia: State RU |
| `nasa-hsf` | institutional-us: NASA Commercial Crew/Cargo spacecraft with a commercial owner |
| `gov-owner-class` | institutional-*: lead owner org class C/D |
| `gov-payload-class` | institutional-*: lead owner A/B but psatcat payload class C/D |
| `commercial` | open-commercial: remaining class A/B owners |

European institutional = government owners in EU-27, UK, Norway, Switzerland, Iceland, Liechtenstein plus ESA, EU (incl. EUSPA, Copernicus) and EUMETSAT. Other governments = every other state outside US/CN/RU (Japan, India, Korea, Taiwan, Turkey, Israel, ...). Starshield satellites are NRO-owned but SpaceX-built and SpaceX-launched, so they are counted as captive with Starlink (they would be US-captive either way). HK and MO count as China.

**Segments.** `(customer, group, orbit class, 2° inclination bin [lo, hi), mass class)` with `satsPerYear`, `avgMassKg` (mean of the payloads in the segment) and `massKg` (their total).

## Outputs

`dashboard/src/data/generated/demand-<year>.json`:

- `meta`: year, source, url, retrieved, dataVintage, payloads, massKg, massImputedShare (pipeline imputations), massEstimatedShare (McDowell `?` estimates), excluded (counts per reason), failedToOrbitPayloads, customerRules (counts per rule), files, notes
- `totals`: byCustomerGroup, byOrbit, andoyaBand (required), plus byCustomer, openMarket, openMarketLe700, andoyaBandOpenMarket, andoyaBandOpenMarketLe700, andoyaBandByCustomerGroup (each `{sats, massKg}`)
- `segments`: `{id, label, group, orbit, inclination: [lo, hi], massClass, avgMassKg, massKg, customer, satsPerYear}`; `customer` is one of captive, institutional-us, institutional-eu, institutional-other, open-commercial

## Comparison with published totals

McDowell's own annual totals (stats page, same GCAT data) count every standard payload in the orbital catalogs, including payloads of failed or marginal launches that briefly orbited or are listed there (Starship test ships), and sum `TotMass`. Rebuilding that number from the raw files reconciles our population with the published totals:

| Year | This dataset | + failed/marginal launches | = McDowell-style count | Published (McDowell) | Published (brief) | Mass (this, `Mass`) | `TotMass`, McDowell-style set | Published tonnage |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2024 | 2,840 | 24 | 2,864 | 2,864 | ~2,900 (BryceTech/McDowell) | 2,026.0 t | 2,625.4 t | 2,625.9 t |
| 2025 | 4,521 | 5 | 4,526 | 4,526 | ~4,526 (McDowell 2025 summary) | 2,693.7 t | 3,194.0 t | 3,194.0 t |

- 2024: gap to McDowell -24 (-0.8%). Our exclusions he counts: 20 payloads of launches coded as failures (e.g. S60244 Starlink 11214; S60245 Starlink 11202), 4 payloads of marginal launches (Starship flight tests, 'yyyy-Unn'), 0 with a suborbital canonical orbit. Non-standard `PX` objects (12) are excluded by both. Adding them back gives 2,864, exactly the published figure.
- 2025: gap to McDowell -5 (-0.1%). Our exclusions he counts: 1 payloads of launches coded as failures (e.g. A11484 LM-400 Tech Demo), 3 payloads of marginal launches (Starship flight tests, 'yyyy-Unn'), 1 with a suborbital canonical orbit. Non-standard `PX` objects (46) are excluded by both. Adding them back gives 4,526, exactly the published figure.
- The brief's ~2,900 for 2024 is a rounded BryceTech/McDowell figure; BryceTech counts spacecraft with its own rules. McDowell's current stats page and this rebuild agree for both years.
- Mass: our tonnage is lower because we use each payload's own insertion mass (`Mass`) and drop Starship test ships (~120 t each). `TotMass` includes attached objects (crew/cargo ship cargo and trunks, mass simulators, sometimes the upper stage: Viasat's CPS demo is 10 kg `Mass` but 28,010 kg `TotMass`), so it suits tonnage statistics but not per-satellite launch demand.

## Caveats

- Historic flown demand, not a forecast and not a measure of what customers would have bought from a new launcher; captive and institutional rules are policy assumptions.
- 13% of payload masses are McDowell's own estimates (`?`, about ±20 %); secret US/Chinese orbits are partly estimates (`?` flags on perigee/apogee/inclination).
- Canonical orbits are early-operations orbits; Starlink canonical orbits are often insertion orbits (inclination is unaffected).
- SatType flags are hand-entered and occasionally misplaced (deep-space flag on the LEO cubesat Tenkoh-2, station-deploy flag in different bytes in 2024 and 2025); the pipeline therefore relies on orbit data, Status and piece names rather than single flag bytes.
- Owner classes come from GCAT's organisation table; state-owned companies (e.g. NSIL, Space Norway, Turksat) count as commercial unless McDowell marks the payload itself as civil/defence.
- Launches late in a year whose payloads are deployed later (ISS cargo) count in the launch year; objects not yet catalogued at the data vintage are missing (small for 2025 as of 2026-10).
